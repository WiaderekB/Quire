\begin{abstract}
  A short note on how much we notice when we give one
  thing our full attention. We compare two reading
  conditions using a simple recall task.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Attention is easier to describe than to measure. In
this note, we ask whether reading in a quiet setting
changes what a reader remembers.

\section{Method}
Participants read the same passage in two conditions.
After each reading, they record the details they
remember. We score each response as follows:
\[
  R = \frac{\text{details recalled}}
           {\text{details in passage}}
\]
